solid vs engineered wood flooring: a technical guide for delhi ncr

the most common misconception is that engineered wood automatically shortens the interior execution timeline India because it is “pre‑made”. In reality, the speed of material execution depends on how the product reacts to the local climate, sub‑floor conditions, and installation method. This guide breaks down the two options – solid wood and engineered wood – so you can see which fits your Delhi NCR interior design project and budget of ₹40–80 lakh.

material properties

Both flooring types start with a wood veneer, but their internal structure diverges.

solid wood

  • single piece of timber, usually 18–25 mm thick
  • natural grain runs through the entire board
  • expands and contracts with humidity changes
  • can be sanded and refinished 3–5 times depending on thickness

engineered wood

  • multiple layers: a top veneer (usually 2–4 mm) bonded to plywood or high‑density fiberboard core
  • layers are cross‑laminated to improve dimensional stability
  • limited sanding – typically only once or twice
  • designed to resist warping in humid environments

For Delhi NCR, where seasonal humidity swings from 30 % in winter to 70 % in monsoon, engineered wood’s cross‑laminated core offers better stability, while solid wood can develop gaps if not acclimatized properly.

cost difference

Cost is often the first factor clients compare. Below is an illustrative price range for a 200 sq m home, fitting the ₹40–80 lakh budget.

  • Solid wood: ₹2,200–₹3,000 per sq ft, total ₹48–₹66 lakh for 2,200 sq ft.
  • Engineered wood: ₹1,500–₹2,200 per sq ft, total ₹33–₹48 lakh for the same area.

These figures include material, underlay, and standard installation. The lower material cost of engineered wood can free up funds for premium finishes or custom millwork, but the savings may be offset by additional sub‑floor preparation.

durability, moisture & termite behaviour

Both options are susceptible to moisture, but they react differently.

moisture resistance

Engineered wood’s layered construction makes it less prone to cupping and crowning when exposed to sudden humidity changes. Solid wood, if not properly sealed, can swell, especially in basements or ground‑level rooms where moisture ingress is common.

termite resistance

Termite activity is a real concern in Delhi NCR. Solid wood provides a uniform food source, while engineered wood’s core often contains treated plywood or MDF that is less attractive to termites. Nonetheless, both require a chemical barrier during material execution to meet local building codes.

wear and tear

In high‑traffic zones, engineered wood with a hard‑wear top layer (e.g., aluminum oxide finish) can outperform solid wood that may show scratches sooner. However, the ability to refinish solid wood multiple times can extend its service life if you’re willing to invest in periodic sanding.

where each option makes sense

Choosing the right floor depends on the room function, client expectations, and project timeline.

solid wood – ideal scenarios

  • heritage homes where authentic timber character is a priority.
  • rooms with stable climate control, such as air‑conditioned living areas.
  • clients who value the ability to refinish the floor over decades.

engineered wood – ideal scenarios

  • new constructions in Delhi NCR where the sub‑floor may have minor unevenness.
  • areas prone to humidity, such as kitchens, bathrooms (with proper sealing), or basements.
  • tight project schedules – the lighter boards and click‑lock systems can reduce installation time by up to 30 %.

indéva studio typically specifies engineered wood as the default for most Delhi NCR interior design projects because it balances cost, stability, and a reasonable execution timeline. We still recommend solid wood for clients who place historic authenticity above speed.

frequently asked questions

Q1: Can I install solid wood over a concrete slab?

Yes, but the slab must be level and a moisture barrier applied. A floating sub‑floor of plywood is often recommended to allow the wood to breathe.

Q2: How often should engineered wood be refinished?

Typically once, unless the top veneer is very thin. Choose a product with a thicker wear layer (≥4 mm) if you anticipate multiple refinishes.

Q3: Does engineered wood need a separate termite treatment?

Even though the core is less attractive to termites, a perimeter chemical barrier is required by local regulations for any wood product.

Q4: Which flooring is more eco‑friendly?

Engineered wood often uses less high‑grade timber and can incorporate recycled core layers, making it a greener choice when sourced responsibly.
