interior BOQ vs quotation: technical guide for Delhi NCR homes

Most first‑time owners believe an interior BOQ and a quotation are interchangeable documents. In reality, they serve distinct purposes: a BOQ (Bill of Quantities) lists every material, size, and finish required for a project, while a quotation translates that list into a price offer from a contractor. Understanding the split prevents surprise costs and keeps a ₹15–25 lakh budget on track.

material properties

When preparing a material boq, we assess each component on its intrinsic qualities. Below are the most common categories used in Delhi NCR interior design:

  • Solid wood (teak, sheesham) – high tensile strength, natural grain, good dimensional stability but susceptible to moisture if untreated.
  • Engineered wood (MDF, particle board) – uniform density, easy to finish, lower cost, but weaker in high‑humidity zones.
  • Natural stone (marble, granite) – excellent compressive strength, heat resistant, heavy; requires proper sealing to avoid staining.
  • Porcelain tiles – low water absorption, high abrasion resistance, ideal for bathrooms and kitchens.
  • Metal fixtures (stainless steel, brass) – corrosion‑resistant, strong, but can be pricey.

Each material’s performance influences the quantities we record in the boq. For example, a solid‑wood wardrobe may need a 10 % waste allowance because of cutting losses, whereas porcelain tiles typically include a 5 % overrun for breakage.

cost difference

The quotation aggregates the material boq with labor rates, equipment charges, and contractor margins. The same material list can generate vastly different quotes depending on:

  1. Source of supply – Direct factory purchase versus middle‑man can swing prices by 10‑20 %.
  2. Labor skill level – Master carpenters charge higher rates but reduce re‑work, affecting overall cost.
  3. Project timing – Seasonal demand peaks (e.g., festive season) often push rates up.

In an illustrative budget of ₹20 lakh for a 2,500 sq ft home, the material boq might account for ₹11 lakh (≈55 %). The remaining ₹9 lakh in the quotation typically covers installation, finishing, and contingency. Understanding this split helps homeowners decide where to allocate extra funds—whether to upgrade to a premium stone or to add extra insulation.

durability, moisture and termite behaviour

Delhi NCR experiences a monsoon season with high humidity, and termite activity is common in older parts of the city. Material selection therefore matters not just for aesthetics but for long‑term performance.

Wood

Solid hardwood, when properly seasoned and treated with a polyurethane sealant, can resist moisture ingress for 10‑12 years. Engineered wood, however, expands noticeably if exposed to water, leading to joint failure. Termite resistance is higher in hardwood due to its natural oils, but chemical treatment is still advisable.

Stone and tiles

Natural stone is virtually impervious to termites and does not swell with humidity. Porcelain tiles have a water absorption rate below 0.5 %, making them ideal for bathrooms. The key risk is improper grouting, which can trap moisture and cause mold.

Metals

Stainless steel fixtures are corrosion‑free in humid climates, while brass may develop a patina if not maintained. Both are termite‑proof.

Choosing a material solely on price can backfire; a cheaper engineered‑wood ceiling may need replacement in five years, costing more than a premium solid‑wood option that lasts a decade.

when each option makes sense

Deciding whether to rely on a detailed material boq or a simple quotation depends on project complexity and client involvement.

High‑visibility areas (living room, master bedroom)

Use a full material boq. Precise quantities and finish specifications allow the client to visualise the final look and control costs tightly.

Utility zones (service area, pantry)

A quotation based on standard rates may suffice. These zones have less aesthetic demand, so a generic cost estimate speeds up execution.

Renovations with structural changes

A material boq becomes essential to coordinate with structural engineers, especially when new load‑bearing walls are introduced.

Turnkey packages offered by contractors

Some contractors provide a lump‑sum quotation that already includes a built‑in material boq. In such cases, verify that the included quantities match your design intent, otherwise ask for a separate material boq for transparency.

  • Quality control – each item is vetted for durability in Delhi NCR’s climate.

frequently asked questions

Q1: Can I skip the material boq and go straight to a quotation?

A1: You can, but you lose visibility into exact quantities and may face unexpected price escalations later. A material boq acts as a safeguard, especially for bespoke elements.

Q2: How does a material boq help with termite protection?

A2: By listing each wood component, we can specify termite‑resistant treatments for every item. The quotation then includes the cost of those treatments, keeping the budget transparent.

Q3: Are there any materials that should never be used in Delhi NCR homes?

A3: Untreated solid wood in high‑moisture areas (like bathrooms) is risky. Similarly, low‑grade MDF without moisture‑resistant coating can warp quickly.

Q4: What if the contractor’s quotation exceeds my budget?

A4: Compare the quoted rates with the material boq. If labour rates are high, negotiate or seek alternative contractors. You can also adjust material specifications—perhaps swapping a premium stone for a high‑quality tile—to bring costs down.
