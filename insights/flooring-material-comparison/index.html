Choosing the Right Flooring for Indian Homes

One of the most common misconceptions about flooring is that it's all about aesthetics. While the look and feel of your flooring is important, it's not the only factor to consider. When it comes to choosing the best flooring material for Indian homes, there are several technical factors to take into account, including durability, moisture resistance, and termite behavior.

Material Properties

Let's start by comparing the properties of two popular flooring materials: marble and hardwood. Marble is a natural stone that is known for its beauty and durability. It's resistant to heat and cold, but can be prone to etching and staining. Hardwood, on the other hand, is a natural wood that is known for its warmth and character. It's durable and can last for decades with proper maintenance, but can be sensitive to moisture and termite damage.

Cost Difference

The cost of flooring materials can vary widely, depending on the type and quality of the material. For a home in Delhi NCR with a budget of ₹1-3 crore, the cost of flooring can range from ₹50 to ₹200 per square foot, depending on the material chosen. Marble, for example, can cost upwards of ₹100 per square foot, while hardwood can cost between ₹50 to ₹150 per square foot.

Moisture and Termite Behavior

Moisture and termite damage are two of the biggest concerns when it comes to flooring in Indian homes. Marble is generally resistant to moisture damage, but can be prone to staining and etching. Hardwood, on the other hand, is sensitive to moisture and can be damaged by termite infestations. However, there are ways to protect your hardwood flooring from moisture and termite damage, such as using a waterproof sealant and regular maintenance.

Where Each Option Makes Sense

The choice of flooring material ultimately depends on your personal preferences and the specific needs of your home. Marble is a good choice for high-traffic areas, such as living rooms and hallways, while hardwood is better suited for bedrooms and study areas. At indéva studio, we typically specify marble for high-end projects with a budget of ₹2-3 crore, while hardwood is often used for more budget-friendly projects with a budget of ₹1-2 crore.

Default Specifications

At indéva studio, we default to specifying high-quality flooring materials that meet our technical standards. For marble, we typically specify a minimum thickness of 18mm and a polished finish. For hardwood, we specify a minimum thickness of 15mm and a UV-cured finish. We also recommend regular maintenance and upkeep to ensure the longevity of the flooring material.

It's not just about choosing the right material, it's about understanding how it will perform in the Indian climate and taking steps to protect it from damage.

For homeowners in Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR, it's especially important to consider the local climate and environmental conditions when choosing a flooring material. With the right material and proper maintenance, your flooring can last for decades and provide a beautiful and durable surface for your home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best flooring material for a home with high traffic?

Marble is a good choice for high-traffic areas, such as living rooms and hallways, due to its durability and resistance to wear and tear.

How do I protect my hardwood flooring from moisture damage?

Regular maintenance, such as sweeping and mopping, can help protect your hardwood flooring from moisture damage. You can also use a waterproof sealant to protect the wood from spills and stains.

What is the average cost of flooring materials in India?

The cost of flooring materials in India can vary widely, depending on the type and quality of the material. On average, the cost of flooring materials can range from ₹50 to ₹200 per square foot.

Can I use marble flooring in a bathroom or kitchen?

Yes, marble can be used in bathrooms and kitchens, but it requires special care and maintenance to prevent staining and etching. It's recommended to use a sealant and clean up spills immediately to protect the marble.
