luxury interior designer greater kailash: realistic cost guide for homes

published september 2026 · updated 2 november 2026 · 4 min read · indéva studio

Updated 2 November 2026: the room-by-room items now add up to the illustrative budget table, the table total is shown worked out, and an unsourced survey figure, an unattributed quotation and an unverified discount policy have been removed.

Many homeowners assume a luxury interior makeover starts at ₹1 crore. It need not: this guide works through an illustrative budget for a Greater Kailash duplex in the tens of lakhs, and shows where the money goes.

what drives costs up

Understanding why a design budget can balloon is the first step toward control. In the South Delhi locality of greater kailash, three factors dominate:

  • Material premium: Imported marble, custom brass fittings, and hand‑woven fabrics carry a hefty price tag. Locally sourced alternatives usually cost less — compare both options line by line.
  • Design complexity: Intricate wall paneling, bespoke furniture, and integrated lighting systems demand more labour hours and specialised craftsmen.
  • Project scope creep: Adding rooms, changing colour palettes mid‑way, or upgrading fixtures without revisiting the contract inflates costs.

When a client engages a luxury interior design company Greater Kailash, transparent cost structures become essential. A reputable firm will provide a detailed estimate before any work begins, allowing homeowners to compare line items against their own priorities.

cost breakdown by room

Below is an illustrative allocation for a duplex of about 2,500 sq ft in Greater Kailash, Delhi. The figures are made up to show how a budget is built — not market rates — and in this example they add up to about ₹22–39 lakh (see the table further down). Each room’s items add up to its row in that table.

living area

Living spaces often take the largest share because they set the tone for the home — about 31–34% of the total in this example.

  • Flooring (engineered wood or premium tiles): ₹3–5 lakh
  • Custom sofa set and coffee table: ₹2–3 lakh
  • Lighting (chandeliers, recessed LEDs): ₹1–2 lakh
  • Wall finishes (paint, textured panels): ₹1.5–2 lakh

kitchen

Modern kitchens balance looks with function — about 26–27% of the total here.

  • Cabinetry (solid wood with soft‑close hinges): ₹3–5 lakh
  • Countertops (quartz or natural stone): ₹1–2 lakh
  • Appliances (built‑in oven, induction cooktop): ₹1.5–2.5 lakh
  • Plumbing fixtures and lighting: ₹0.5 lakh

bedrooms (two, together)

Bedrooms usually get a more restrained treatment — about 18–21% of the total here.

  • Wardrobe systems: ₹2–4 lakh
  • Flooring and wall accents: ₹1–2 lakh
  • Bedding & soft furnishings: ₹0.5–1 lakh
  • Ambient lighting: ₹0.5–1 lakh

bathrooms (two, together)

Wet areas can cost more than their size suggests because of waterproofing and sanitaryware — about 9–10% of the total here.

  • Vanity units & mirrors: ₹0.5–1 lakh
  • Tiles (floor & wall): ₹0.75–1.5 lakh
  • Premium faucets & fittings: ₹0.5–1 lakh
  • Ventilation & lighting: ₹0.25–0.5 lakh

how to negotiate

Negotiation is not about cheapening the design; it is about aligning value with budget. Follow these steps:

  1. Request a line‑item quote: A detailed breakdown lets you see where adjustments are possible.
  2. Prioritise must‑haves: Identify non‑negotiable elements (e.g., structural changes) and be flexible on finishes.
  3. Leverage bulk ordering: If multiple rooms use the same material, ask for a volume discount.
  4. Set milestone payments: Linking payments to completed phases protects both parties.

red flags that inflate bills

Even seasoned homeowners can fall prey to hidden expenses. Keep an eye out for these warning signs:

The most common source of surprise charges is the lack of a clear change‑order process: without one, every small tweak becomes a billable event.

  • Vague scope statements that omit “what’s not included.”
  • Unexplained escalation clauses tied to material price fluctuations.
  • Frequent on‑site revisions without documented approvals.
  • Absence of a signed schedule of rates for labour.

When any of these appear, pause and request clarification before authorising further work.

frequently asked questions

Q1? How accurate are budget estimates for luxury projects?

The figures in this guide are illustrative. A real estimate is only as accurate as its BOQ: once every item has a fixed material, brand and rate, the remaining variation comes mainly from price changes and design changes you ask for.

Q2? Can I source my own materials to reduce costs?

Yes, but the designer must approve each item to ensure compatibility with the overall scheme.

Q3? What is the typical timeline for a ₹30 lakh duplex project?

It depends on scope, approvals and material lead times; structural work adds time. Ask for a written schedule with milestones.

Q4? How does interior design in south delhi differ from other zones?

Many South Delhi homes, including in Greater Kailash, are older buildings. Their structure, wiring and plumbing may need checking before new work, which can affect both cost and schedule.
