luxury interior designer greater kailash: realistic cost guide for homes

september 2026 · 4 min read · indéva studio

A recent survey shows that 62% of Delhi homeowners believe a luxury interior makeover starts at ₹1 crore, yet most projects in Greater Kailash finish well below that threshold.

what drives costs up

Understanding why a design budget can balloon is the first step toward control. In the south_delhi locality greater kailash, three factors dominate:

  • Material premium: Imported marble, custom brass fittings, and hand‑woven fabrics carry a hefty price tag. Choosing locally sourced alternatives can shave 15‑20% off the total.
  • Design complexity: Intricate wall paneling, bespoke furniture, and integrated lighting systems demand more labour hours and specialised craftsmen.
  • Project scope creep: Adding rooms, changing colour palettes mid‑way, or upgrading fixtures without revisiting the contract inflates costs.

When a client engages a luxury interior design company Greater Kailash, transparent cost structures become essential. A reputable firm will provide a detailed estimate before any work begins, allowing homeowners to compare line items against their own priorities.

cost breakdown by room

Below is a typical allocation for a duplex spanning 2,500 sq ft in Greater Kailash, Delhi. Figures are illustrative and reflect a budget range of ₹25–50 lakh.

living area

Living spaces often consume the largest share because they set the tone for the entire home. Expect 30‑35% of the total budget here.

  • Flooring (engineered wood or premium tiles): ₹4‑6 lakh
  • Custom sofa set and coffee table: ₹2‑3 lakh
  • Lighting (chandeliers, recessed LEDs): ₹1‑2 lakh
  • Wall finishes (paint, textured panels): ₹1‑1.5 lakh

kitchen

Modern kitchens balance aesthetics with functionality. A well‑planned kitchen typically accounts for 20‑25% of the overall spend.

  • Cabinetry (solid wood with soft‑close hinges): ₹3‑5 lakh
  • Countertops (quartz or natural stone): ₹2‑3 lakh
  • Appliances (built‑in oven, induction cooktop): ₹2‑3 lakh
  • Plumbing fixtures and lighting: ₹0.5‑1 lakh

bedrooms

Private chambers receive a more restrained treatment, usually 15‑20% of the total.

  • Wardrobe systems: ₹2‑4 lakh
  • Flooring and wall accents: ₹1‑2 lakh
  • Bedding & soft furnishings: ₹0.5‑1 lakh
  • Ambient lighting: ₹0.5‑1 lakh

bathrooms & utility

These functional zones can be surprisingly costly due to waterproofing and premium sanitary ware. Allocate 10‑15% of the budget.

  • Vanity units & mirrors: ₹1‑2 lakh
  • Tiles (floor & wall): ₹1‑2 lakh
  • Premium faucets & fittings: ₹0.5‑1 lakh
  • Ventilation & lighting: ₹0.5‑1 lakh

how to negotiate

Negotiation is not about cheapening the design; it is about aligning value with budget. Follow these steps:

  1. Request a line‑item quote: A detailed breakdown lets you see where adjustments are possible.
  2. Prioritise must‑haves: Identify non‑negotiable elements (e.g., structural changes) and be flexible on finishes.
  3. Leverage bulk ordering: If multiple rooms use the same material, ask for a volume discount.
  4. Set milestone payments: Linking payments to completed phases protects both parties.

indéva studio often suggests a 10% discount on material costs when the client agrees to a fixed‑price contract for the entire duplex.

red flags that inflate bills

Even seasoned homeowners can fall prey to hidden expenses. Keep an eye out for these warning signs:

“The most common source of surprise charges is the lack of a clear change‑order protocol. Without it, every minor tweak becomes a billable event.” – senior project manager, indéva studio
  • Vague scope statements that omit “what’s not included.”
  • Unexplained escalation clauses tied to material price fluctuations.
  • Frequent on‑site revisions without documented approvals.
  • Absence of a signed schedule of rates for labour.

When any of these appear, pause and request clarification before authorising further work.

Estimates are based on current market rates for materials and labour in the Greater Kailash area. A ±10% variance is typical due to price changes or client‑initiated design tweaks.
