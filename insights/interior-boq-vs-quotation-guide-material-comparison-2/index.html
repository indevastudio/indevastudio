understanding interior boq vs quotation for delhi ncr homes

september 2026 · 5 min read · indéva studio

The most common misconception is that an interior BOQ and a quotation are interchangeable documents; in reality, they serve distinct technical purposes in a Delhi NCR interior design project.

what are interior BOQ and quotation?

A Bill of Quantities (BOQ) lists every material, finish, and fixture required for a space, along with precise measurements and specifications. It is a quantitative inventory used mainly for cost estimation and tendering. A quotation, on the other hand, is a price offer from a contractor or supplier that aggregates the BOQ items into a single monetary figure, often adding labor, overhead, and contingency charges. While both aim to control budget, the BOQ provides the granular data, whereas the quotation translates that data into a payable amount.

why material BOQ matters

A material BOQ records exact dimensions, weight, and finish codes. This level of detail ensures that the subsequent quotation reflects realistic procurement costs and avoids hidden surcharges.

cost difference analysis

Cost variance between a BOQ and its quotation can be significant, especially in a project with an illustrative budget of ₹1–3 crore. The BOQ itself carries no price; it merely lists quantities. The quotation adds unit rates, labor, transportation, and contingency.

  • Unit rates: Market‑driven prices for each material (e.g., ₹2,500 per sq.ft. for premium marble).
  • Labor: Skilled carpentry, masonry, and finishing labor typically range from 15 % to 25 % of material cost.
  • Overheads & profit: Contractors include a margin of 8 %–12 % to cover administration and risk.
  • Contingency: A prudent quotation adds 5 %–10 % to absorb price fluctuations.

For an illustrative budget of ₹2 crore, a well‑prepared BOQ may show material costs of ₹1.2 crore. Applying the typical percentages above, the final quotation could rise to ₹1.8–₹2.0 crore, illustrating why the two documents must be reviewed together.

durability, moisture, and termite behaviour

Technical specifications in the BOQ also address long‑term performance. The following considerations are crucial for Delhi NCR’s climate, which experiences high humidity and occasional termite activity.

moisture resistance

  • Solid wood: Requires proper sealing; otherwise, it swells in humid conditions.
  • Engineered wood: Better dimensional stability; less prone to warping.
  • Stone: Naturally moisture‑resistant, but requires sealants on polished surfaces.
  • Metal: Non‑porous, but must be treated to prevent corrosion in coastal pockets of NCR.

termite resistance

Termite susceptibility is primarily a concern for timber elements. The BOQ can specify treated wood or alternative materials such as metal or stone for structural components. A quotation that ignores these specifications may result in higher maintenance costs later.

when each document makes sense

Understanding the appropriate use‑case for a BOQ versus a quotation helps clients make informed decisions.

  • Early design phase: A BOQ guides material selection and assists architects in visualising space.
  • Tendering phase: Contractors submit quotations based on the BOQ, enabling transparent price comparison.
  • Execution phase: The final quotation becomes the contract document; any deviation from the BOQ must be formally approved.

In projects where budget certainty is critical—such as a ₹2.5‑crore luxury residence in Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR—starting with a detailed BOQ reduces the risk of surprise cost escalations.

frequently asked questions

Q1? How detailed should a material BOQ be for a high‑end residential project?

A material BOQ should capture every quantifiable item—dimensions, finish codes, and reference standards. For a ₹1–3 crore budget, this level of detail prevents cost overruns of 10 % or more.

Q2? Can a quotation be negotiated after the BOQ is finalized?

Yes. Since the BOQ fixes quantities, negotiations focus on unit rates, labor efficiency, and contingency percentages. However, any change in quantities requires a revised BOQ.

Q3? What role does a material BOQ play in managing moisture‑related issues?

The BOQ specifies moisture‑resistant finishes (e.g., treated timber, sealed stone). By selecting appropriate materials early, the quotation can include the correct labor and product costs, avoiding future remedial expenses.

Q4? How does indéva studio ensure that quotations reflect true market rates?

indéva studio cross‑references supplier price lists, monitors market trends, and updates its rate database quarterly. This practice, combined with transparent markup, builds client trust.
