living room tv unit design ideas india: a definitive cost guide

did you know that a significant number of indian homeowners overspend on their living room tv unit design by up to 30%? this is often due to a lack of understanding of the costs involved and not having a clear budget in mind. in this article, we will break down the costs associated with living room tv unit design and provide you with cost-effective ideas for your heritage bungalow in Lutyens Delhi, Delhi, with a budget of ₹8-15 lakh.

what drives costs up in living room tv unit design?

several factors can drive up the cost of living room tv unit design, including the type of materials used, the complexity of the design, and the location of your bungalow. for example, a tv unit design that incorporates expensive materials like solid wood or marble can cost upwards of ₹2-3 lakh, while a simpler design using mdf or plywood can cost significantly less, around ₹50,000-1 lakh.

cost breakdown by room

when it comes to living room tv unit design, the costs can be broken down into several components, including:

  • material costs: ₹1-3 lakh
  • labor costs: ₹50,000-1 lakh
  • design and consultation fees: ₹20,000-50,000
  • electrical and lighting costs: ₹10,000-30,000

how to negotiate with your interior designer

when negotiating with your interior designer, it's essential to have a clear understanding of your budget and what you want to achieve with your design. don't be afraid to ask questions and seek clarification on any costs or design elements that you're unsure about. as a member of the indian green building council, we at indéva studio are committed to providing our clients with sustainable and cost-effective design solutions.

red flags that inflate bills

there are several red flags that can inflate your bill, including:

  • hidden costs: make sure you understand all the costs involved in your design, including any additional fees or charges.
  • overdesigning: be wary of designers who try to oversell you on expensive materials or design elements that you don't need.
  • poor communication: make sure you have a clear understanding of your design and any changes that are made during the process.
