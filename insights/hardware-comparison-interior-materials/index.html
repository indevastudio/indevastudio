choosing the right hardware: a technical guide for interiors

september 2026 · 5 min read · indéva studio

The most common misconception about selecting fixtures is that price alone decides quality; in reality, material performance, environment, and design intent matter just as much.

material properties: brass vs stainless steel vs aluminium

When a designer talks about best hardware brands for interiors, the conversation usually narrows to three widely available material families:

  • Brass – an alloy of copper and zinc, known for its warm golden hue and natural antimicrobial properties.
  • Stainless steel – an iron‑chromium alloy that resists corrosion and maintains a sleek, modern look.
  • Aluminium – a lightweight metal with excellent formability, often finished with anodising or powder coating.

Physical characteristics

Brass is denser (≈8.5 g/cm³) than stainless steel (≈8.0 g/cm³) and aluminium (≈2.7 g/cm³). This density difference influences how a handle feels in the hand and how much torque it can withstand before deforming.

Stainless steel’s chromium layer forms a passive oxide film that protects against rust, while aluminium’s natural oxide layer is thinner and usually enhanced through anodising to improve wear resistance.

Design language

Brass contributes a classic, heritage feel, complementing traditional woodwork or marble. Stainless steel aligns with contemporary minimalism, and aluminium suits lightweight, industrial or avant‑garde interiors.

cost difference

Budget constraints in a typical Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR luxury renovation fall within an illustrative budget of ₹25–50 lakh. Hardware cost is a small but visible line item.

On average:

  • Brass fittings range from ₹250 to ₹800 per piece, depending on finish and size.
  • Stainless steel fittings sit between ₹200 and ₹600 per piece.
  • Aluminium fittings are the most economical, usually ₹150 to ₹400 per piece.

These figures exclude installation labour, which can add 15‑20 % to the total hardware expense. The price gap narrows when premium finishes or custom designs are required, as brass and stainless steel can both be machined to intricate patterns.

durability, moisture & termite behaviour

Delhi NCR experiences high humidity during monsoon and occasional termite activity in older structures. Understanding how each material reacts helps avoid premature replacement.

Corrosion resistance

Stainless steel (especially grades 304 and 316) offers the highest corrosion resistance, making it suitable for kitchens, bathrooms, and outdoor terraces. Brass develops a patina over time; while aesthetically pleasing to some, it may require periodic polishing in high‑moisture zones.

Moisture absorption

Aluminium does not absorb water but can suffer from galvanic corrosion if in direct contact with copper‑based materials without proper isolation. Using insulated washers prevents this issue.

Termite impact

Termites attack organic matter; metal hardware itself is not a food source. However, fasteners that embed into wood can create pathways for infestation. Choosing stainless steel or aluminium screws with corrosion‑resistant coating reduces the risk of wood decay at attachment points.

where each option makes sense

Choosing the right material depends on the specific room, usage intensity, and visual goals.

kitchen and bathroom

Stainless steel is the default choice for high‑traffic wet areas. Its non‑porous surface resists stains and can be cleaned with mild detergents without affecting finish.

living room and bedroom

Brass adds warmth to lounge spaces. In a high‑end villa where the client desires a touch of heritage, brass door handles and lighting fixtures become focal points.

balconies and external facades

Aluminium, when anodised, offers a lightweight solution that tolerates wind loads and requires minimal maintenance. It is also cost‑effective for large‑scale applications like railings.

mixed‑use spaces

Combining materials can create visual hierarchy—brass for primary doors, stainless steel for secondary fixtures, and aluminium for decorative hardware.

frequently asked questions

Q1? How does finish affect the lifespan of brass hardware?

Polished brass maintains its shine longer but can develop a patina in humid environments. Regular cleaning with a mild acidic solution and a protective wax coat can extend its aesthetic life by several years.

Q2? Is stainless steel always the safest choice for wet areas?

While stainless steel resists rust, low‑grade alloys may still show staining if water is left standing. Selecting grade 304 or higher and ensuring proper drainage mitigates this risk.

Q3? Can aluminium hardware be used on wooden doors without causing corrosion?

Yes, provided an insulating barrier—such as a nylon washer—separates the aluminium from the wood. This prevents galvanic action and protects the wood from moisture ingress.

Q4? How do hardware choices impact overall project cost in a ₹25–50 lakh budget?

Hardware typically accounts for 2‑4 % of total interior spend. Selecting stainless steel for functional areas and brass for statement pieces can keep costs within the target range while delivering a premium feel.
