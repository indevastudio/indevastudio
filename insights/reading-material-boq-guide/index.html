reading material boqs: delhi ncr home design guide and cost planning

september 2026 · 5 min read · indéva studio

The most common misconception is that a BOQ (Bill of Quantities) simply lists prices; in reality, it is a detailed map of quantities, specifications, and performance criteria that drives every design decision.

material properties

When a homeowner in Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR, evaluates the interior of a new residence, two material families frequently appear on the material boq: natural marble and engineered quartz. Both are silica‑based stone surfaces, yet their physical characteristics differ enough to affect selection.

composition and finish

  • Natural marble: Formed over millions of years, marble is a metamorphic rock composed mainly of calcite. Its surface can be honed, polished, or brushed, each finish revealing distinct veining patterns.
  • Engineered quartz: Produced by binding crushed quartz crystals with resin and pigments, engineered quartz offers a uniform appearance. It can mimic marble, but the veining is printed rather than natural.

weight and installation

Marble slabs typically weigh 2,500–2,800 kg per cubic meter, requiring reinforced subfloors and heavier handling equipment. Engineered quartz is lighter, around 2,300 kg per cubic meter, allowing standard joists to support it in most residential settings.

cost difference

Budget is a decisive factor, especially within an illustrative budget ₹8–15 lakh for a full‑house interior finish. The cost structure of each material reflects raw material availability, processing, and transport.

price per square foot

  • Natural marble: ₹300–₹600 per sq ft, varying with rarity and finish.
  • Engineered quartz: ₹250–₹450 per sq ft, with a tighter price band due to mass production.

When the BOQ quantifies a 2,000 sq ft flooring area, the total material cost could range from ₹5 lakh for basic quartz to ₹12 lakh for premium marble. Installation labor for marble may add another ₹2–₹3 lakh because of extra handling and alignment requirements.

durability, moisture and termite behaviour

Both materials are non‑porous, yet they react differently to Delhi’s seasonal humidity and the occasional termite presence in older structures.

moisture resistance

Marble is slightly more absorbent, with a water absorption rate of 0.2–0.5 %. In high‑humidity rooms, this can lead to faint staining if sealants are not maintained. Engineered quartz, sealed within its resin matrix, typically absorbs less than 0.1 %, making it a safer choice for kitchens and bathrooms.

scratch and impact resistance

Quartz’s resin binder provides a tougher surface against minor scratches, whereas marble can etch under acidic spills. However, marble’s natural crystalline structure offers superior heat resistance, tolerating hot pans placed directly on the surface for short periods.

termite considerations

Termites do not feed on stone, but they may damage supporting timber. Because marble is heavier, it often requires a sturdier substrate, potentially reducing the amount of wood exposed to termite activity. Quartz, being lighter, may be installed over thinner joists, which could be more vulnerable if the substructure is not properly treated.

where each option makes sense

Choosing between marble and quartz hinges on functional needs, aesthetic goals, and long‑term maintenance willingness.

luxury aesthetics

For clients seeking authentic veining and a classic Indian palate, marble aligns with traditional houzz india showcases of heritage homes. Its unique patterns cannot be duplicated, adding perceived value.

practical durability

If the project timeline is tight and the homeowner prefers low‑maintenance surfaces, engineered quartz wins. Its consistent colour and resistance to staining simplify daily cleaning, ideal for high‑traffic areas like entry foyers.

budget alignment

Within the ₹8–15 lakh range, a balanced mix is possible: marble in the master bedroom and living area for visual impact, while quartz serves the kitchen and utility spaces where spills are frequent.

indéva studio's default specifications and rationale

indéva studio follows a pragmatic approach when drafting a how to read an interior design BOQ. The firm’s standard specification for stone flooring includes:

  • Engineered quartz as the baseline material for all wet zones, due to its proven moisture resistance and predictable cost.

frequently asked questions

What does a material BOQ typically include?

A material BOQ lists each item’s description, unit of measure, quantity, unit rate, and total cost, along with specifications such as finish, grade, and installation method.

How can a homeowner verify the accuracy of a BOQ?

Cross‑check the quantities against the floor plan, request sample specifications, and confirm that the rates reflect current market prices in Delhi NCR.

Is engineered quartz always cheaper than marble?

Not necessarily. While quartz has a tighter price band, high‑end designer quartz can approach the cost of premium marble, especially when custom colours are required.

Can the BOQ be adjusted after design finalisation?

Yes. Any change in material, finish, or quantity should be reflected in a revised BOQ to keep the budget transparent and avoid surprise costs.
