kitchen cabinet material comparison: guide for delhi ncr homes

september 2026 · 5 min read · indéva studio

The biggest myth about kitchen cabinets is that all materials perform the same under Indian climate; a detailed kitchen cabinet material comparison quickly proves otherwise.

material properties

When evaluating cabinets, three material families dominate the Indian market: solid wood, engineered wood (often called MDF or particleboard with veneer), and metal (primarily stainless steel). Each family exhibits distinct physical traits that affect aesthetics, machining, and long‑term behaviour.

solid wood

  • Natural grain offers unique visual depth and can be stained or painted.
  • Density ranges from 600 kg/m³ (teak) to over 900 kg/m³ (sheesham), giving high load‑bearing capacity.
  • Responsive to humidity; expands and contracts with seasonal changes.

engineered wood

  • Core made from compressed fibers bound with resin; uniform density around 650 kg/m³.
  • Surface finished with a thin veneer or melamine laminate, providing consistent colour.
  • Less prone to warping because the fibers are oriented in multiple directions.

metal cabinets

  • Stainless steel (grade 304) is the most common, offering a smooth, hygienic surface.
  • Thermal conductivity is high, so interior temperature changes quickly with ambient shifts.
  • Weight is significant; a standard 600 mm high cabinet panel can weigh 25 kg.

In Delhi NCR interior design projects, indéva studio often recommends engineered wood for high‑traffic kitchens because its dimensional stability matches the region’s monsoon‑driven humidity swings.

cost difference

Budget is a decisive factor, especially for a home project with an illustrative budget of ₹40–80 lakh. The following price brackets are typical in the Delhi NCR market (prices are per linear foot of cabinet face, excluding hardware):

  • Solid wood: ₹2,500 – ₹4,200. Premium species such as teak push the upper limit.
  • Engineered wood: ₹1,200 – ₹2,800. Veneered MDF sits at the lower end, while high‑pressure laminate finishes are at the higher end.
  • Stainless steel: ₹3,500 – ₹5,500. Custom powder‑coated finishes add extra cost.

For a standard 12‑linear‑foot kitchen run, the total material cost could range from ₹14,400 (engineered wood) to ₹63,000 (solid wood premium). When the overall kitchen fit‑out falls within the ₹40–80 lakh envelope, these differences can influence the allocation for lighting, appliances, or bespoke island worktops.

durability, moisture resistance, and termite behaviour

Delhi’s climate swings from scorching summers to heavy monsoon rains, creating a challenging environment for cabinets.

solid wood

Natural wood contains tannins that can resist moisture, but prolonged exposure without proper sealing leads to swelling. Termite activity is a genuine risk in the region; untreated wood can be vulnerable unless treated with borate or similar preservatives.

engineered wood

The resin‑bonded core is inherently resistant to termite damage because insects cannot digest the synthetic matrix. However, the veneer layer can delaminate if water penetrates seams, making edge sealing essential.

metal

Stainless steel offers excellent moisture resistance and is immune to termites. The main durability concern is denting or scratching, which can be mitigated with brushed finishes and careful handling.

indéva studio’s default specification for most Delhi NCR kitchens is a high‑density MDF core with a melamine or veneer finish, complemented by stainless steel hardware. This hybrid approach balances moisture tolerance, termite safety, and cost efficiency.

where each option makes sense

Choosing the right material depends on the client’s lifestyle, design language, and maintenance expectations.

high‑end residential projects

Clients seeking a timeless, handcrafted look may opt for solid wood, especially if the design calls for exposed grain and traditional joinery. The higher upfront cost is justified when the kitchen serves as a focal point and the homeowner values artisanal detail.

mid‑range modern homes

Engineered wood provides a sleek, uniform appearance that pairs well with contemporary hardware. It is especially suitable for families that cook frequently, as the material tolerates frequent cleaning and occasional splashes without warping.

commercial or open‑plan kitchens

Stainless steel cabinets excel in environments where hygiene and durability are paramount. In a Delhi NCR interior design for a boutique café, metal cabinets can withstand heavy use and are easy to sanitize.

what indéva studio specifies by default and why

Based on extensive field observations across Delhi NCR, indéva studio adopts the following baseline:

  • Core: high‑density MDF (≥ 650 kg/m³) for structural rigidity.
  • Surface: melamine laminate with a matte finish for easy cleaning.
  • Edges: sealed with water‑resistant PVC edge banding to prevent moisture ingress.
  • Hardware: stainless steel hinges and soft‑close drawer slides for longevity.
“In a city where monsoon can linger for months, a sealed engineered wood cabinet often outlives a beautifully grained solid wood one, provided the veneer is high quality.” – senior material consultant, indéva studio

This specification addresses the three most common failure points: humidity‑induced swelling, termite attack, and hardware wear. It also aligns with the ₹40–80 lakh budget envelope, allowing funds to be allocated to premium appliances or custom lighting.

frequently asked questions

Q1? How does humidity affect solid wood cabinets in Delhi NCR?

Humidity causes the wood fibres to absorb moisture, leading to expansion. In Delhi’s monsoon season, untreated solid wood can swell up to 2 mm, which may affect door alignment and cause squeaking.

Q2? Is engineered wood safe from termites?

The resin‑bonded core of engineered wood is not a food source for termites, making it largely immune. However, the veneer layer should be sealed to prevent moisture that could attract insects.

Q3? Can stainless steel cabinets be used in a traditional Indian kitchen?

Yes, stainless steel can be integrated as a secondary element—such as a backsplash or pantry doors—while retaining a traditional wood look for the main cabinets.

Q4? What maintenance does each material require?

Solid wood needs periodic oiling or varnish renewal. Engineered wood should be wiped with a damp cloth; avoid abrasive cleaners. Stainless steel benefits from a mild detergent and a soft cloth to prevent streaks.

Choosing the right cabinet material is a balance of aesthetics, performance, and budget.
