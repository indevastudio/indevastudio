HDHMR vs plywood wardrobe: technical guide for Delhi NCR homes

Most homeowners think the choice between HDHMR and plywood is just about price. In reality, the decision hinges on material behavior, moisture resistance, and long‑term performance, especially in the climate of Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR.

material properties

Both options belong to the engineered‑wood family, but they differ in composition and performance.

HDHMR (high density high moisture resistant)

HDHMR is a medium‑density fibreboard (MDF) that has been treated with a moisture‑resistant resin. The board density typically ranges from 650 kg/m³ to 800 kg/m³, giving it a smooth, uniform surface that accepts laminates, veneers, and paint without noticeable grain.

plywood

Plywood consists of thin wood veneers glued cross‑wise. The layers (or plies) give it high tensile strength and dimensional stability. The face veneer can be natural wood, melamine, or PVC, depending on the finish required.

  • HDHMR: smooth, flat, excellent for high‑gloss finishes.
  • Plywood: natural wood look, better load‑bearing capacity.
  • Both: engineered, less prone to warping than solid wood.

cost difference

Pricing varies with thickness, finish, and supplier, but a typical budget for a built‑in wardrobe in a mid‑range Delhi NCR interior design project falls between ₹8 lakh and ₹15 lakh.

In that range, HDHMR panels usually cost 10‑15 % less per square foot than comparable plywood of the same thickness. The saving can be significant when the wardrobe spans 3 – 4 meters, but the lower material cost may be offset by higher finishing expenses if a premium veneer is desired.

durability, moisture & termite behaviour

Delhi NCR experiences high humidity in monsoon months and dry, hot summers. How the material reacts to these conditions determines its lifespan.

Moisture resistance

HDHMR is specifically formulated to repel moisture; the resin coating reduces water absorption to below 5 % after 24 hours of exposure. Plywood, especially if not marine‑grade, can absorb up to 12 % moisture, leading to swelling at joints.

Termite resistance

Both engineered boards are less attractive to termites than solid timber because the resin and glue act as deterrents. However, untreated plywood that uses low‑grade veneers may still be vulnerable if the surface is damaged.

Impact and load bearing

Plywood’s cross‑laminated structure gives it superior edge strength, making it better suited for heavy hanging rails or sliding doors with metal tracks. HDHMR can handle typical wardrobe loads but may dent under concentrated weight.

Choosing a material solely on upfront cost often ignores the hidden expenses of future repairs, especially in humid climates where moisture‑induced swelling can ruin hardware and finishes.

where each option makes sense

Understanding the use‑case helps narrow the decision.

Ideal scenarios for HDHMR

  • High‑gloss or matte laminated finishes where a perfectly flat surface is required.
  • Budget‑conscious projects staying within the lower end of the ₹8–15 lakh budget.
  • Spaces with limited exposure to direct water splashes, such as interior bedrooms.

Ideal scenarios for plywood

  • Wardrobes with heavy hanging sections, pull‑out drawers, or integrated shoe racks.
  • Clients who prefer a natural wood veneer or want to match other plywood‑based furniture.
  • Projects where long‑term durability outweighs a modest cost premium.

what indéva studio specifies by default and why

In most Delhi NCR interior design assignments, indéva studio specifies HDHMR for the core panels of a wardrobe and uses a thin plywood veneer for the visible faces. This hybrid approach captures the moisture resistance of HDHMR while delivering the tactile warmth of real wood.

We also recommend sealing all internal joints with a moisture‑proof adhesive and installing edge banding that matches the chosen finish. For high‑traffic families, we may upgrade to marine‑grade plywood for the side panels to prevent any potential swelling.

frequently asked questions

Q1? Is HDHMR suitable for sliding wardrobe doors?

Yes, provided the core panel is at least 18 mm thick and the sliding mechanism is mounted on a reinforced frame. The smooth surface aids the glide of rollers.

Q2? Can plywood be painted the same way as HDHMR?

Plywood can be painted, but the grain may show through if the primer is not applied properly. HDHMR offers a more uniform base, reducing the number of coats needed.

Q3? How does the cost of finishing affect the overall budget?

Finishing HDHMR with high‑gloss laminate can cost ₹150–₹250 per sq ft, while a veneer finish on plywood may range ₹200–₹350 per sq ft. The final impact depends on the total surface area of the wardrobe.

Q4? Which material is more environmentally friendly?

Both are engineered from wood residues, but plywood typically uses thinner veneers, meaning less raw timber per panel. However, HDHMR’s resin content may involve chemicals with higher VOCs. Choose certified low‑VOC options when possible.
