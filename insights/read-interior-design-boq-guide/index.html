how to read an interior design boq: delhi ncr home guide

september 2026 · 5 min read · indéva studio

The biggest myth about a BOQ is that it is just a list of prices; in reality, it is a technical roadmap that links design intent to material performance.

material properties

When how to read an interior design BOQ is approached as a material comparison, the first column to examine is the specification of each finish. Below are two common options for wall cladding in a Delhi NCR home:

gypsum board vs. cement board

  • Gypsum board: lightweight, easy to install, smooth finish, fire‑rated (typically 30‑minute rating).
  • Cement board: heavier, higher compressive strength, excellent moisture resistance, suitable for high‑humidity zones.

solid wood vs. engineered MDF

  • Solid wood: natural grain, high tensile strength, expands/contracts with humidity, requires regular polishing.
  • Engineered MDF: uniform density, stable in varied climates, lower cost, surface can be laminated or painted.

These property tables are reflected in the our services documentation as part of the material boq section, ensuring that every line item can be traced back to a performance criterion.

cost difference

Cost is the most visible metric in any BOQ, but the headline figure rarely tells the whole story. For a typical mid‑range home in Delhi NCR, the budget for wall finishes can vary from ₹40 lakh to ₹80 lakh, depending on the chosen materials.

price breakdown

Assuming a 200 sq m wall area:

  • Gypsum board (₹150 per sq ft) ≈ ₹4.5 lakh
  • Cement board (₹250 per sq ft) ≈ ₹7.5 lakh
  • Solid wood paneling (₹500 per sq ft) ≈ ₹15 lakh
  • Engineered MDF (₹300 per sq ft) ≈ ₹9 lakh

The cost gap widens when accessories, joint treatments, and labor are added. A higher upfront spend on cement board can reduce future waterproofing expenses, which is a factor often hidden in the BOQ’s “contingency” line.

durability, moisture & termite behaviour

Durability is not just about lifespan; it includes how a material reacts to Delhi’s monsoon humidity and termite activity common in the region.

moisture resistance

Cement board can absorb up to 5 % moisture without swelling, making it suitable for bathrooms and kitchens. Gypsum board, by contrast, begins to lose integrity at 2 % moisture, so it is best limited to dry zones.

termite resistance

Solid wood requires chemical treatment or a physical barrier to deter termites, adding to the overall cost. Engineered MDF is manufactured with resin binders that are less attractive to termites, though it should still be sealed in high‑risk areas.

where each option makes sense

Choosing the right material depends on functional zones, client budget, and long‑term maintenance plans.

high‑visibility living spaces

For living rooms where aesthetics dominate, gypsum board paired with high‑gloss paint offers a sleek look at a moderate price. If the client prefers a natural texture, solid wood can be justified when the budget leans toward the upper ₹80 lakh range.

wet zones and service areas

Bathrooms, kitchens, and laundry rooms benefit from cement board or waterproofed gypsum. The added cost is offset by reduced risk of water‑induced delamination.

budget‑conscious projects

When the client’s budget aligns with the lower end of the ₹40 lakh bracket, engineered MDF provides a stable, termite‑resistant alternative without compromising on finish quality.

frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an interior BOQ and an interior interiors BOQ?

An interior BOQ lists all items needed for the fit‑out, while an interior interiors BOQ focuses specifically on finishes and decorative elements. Both serve the same purpose of cost control but differ in scope.

How does Delhi NCR interior design influence material selection?

Delhi NCR experiences high humidity in monsoon months and extreme temperature variations. Materials with low hygroscopic expansion, like cement board and engineered MDF, are therefore preferred.

Can I substitute a higher‑priced material without changing the total budget?

Yes, by adjusting quantities or selecting a less expensive complementary finish. Any substitution must be captured in the revised BOQ to keep the cost envelope intact.

Is a material boq mandatory for residential projects?

While not legally required, a material boq provides a clear audit trail, helps avoid disputes, and ensures that the design intent is executed accurately.
