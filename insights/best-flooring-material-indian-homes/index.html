best flooring material for Indian homes – a technical comparison

Many homeowners start their search believing that “the most expensive floor is always the best.” In reality, the right floor depends on climate, usage, and budget, not on price tags alone. This guide looks at the two most common contenders in Delhi NCR interior design – polished vitrified tiles and engineered wood – and evaluates them on the parameters that matter to a technical decision‑maker.

material properties: what each product is made of

Understanding the composition of a floor helps predict how it will behave over time.

Polished vitrified tiles

  • Base: a blend of clay, silica, and feldspar fired at >1200 °C.
  • Surface: a thin glazed layer that gives high gloss and stain resistance.
  • Thickness: typically 8–12 mm for residential use.

Engineered wood flooring

  • Core: multiple layers of high‑density fibreboard (HDF) or plywood bonded with resin.
  • Face veneer: a thin slice (0.6–2 mm) of real hardwood, usually oak, teak, or mango.
  • Finish: polyurethane or water‑based topcoat for wear resistance.

Both materials can be installed over concrete screed, but the bonding method differs. Vitrified tiles use thin‑set mortar, while engineered wood relies on a floating or glue‑down system.

cost difference: budgeting for a typical 200 sq m home

Cost is the most immediate factor for a client with an ILLUSTRATIVE BUDGET of ₹12 lakh for flooring.

• Polished vitrified tiles – ₹90–₹150 per sq ft (≈₹970–₹1,620 per sq m). For 200 sq m, material cost ranges ₹1.94–₹3.24 lakh.

• Engineered wood – ₹250–₹400 per sq ft (≈₹2,690–₹4,300 per sq m). For the same area, material cost is ₹5.38–₹8.60 lakh.

Installation adds roughly 20 % for tiles (grouting, leveling) and 25 % for wood (underlay, acclimatization). When you add the two, vitrified tiles stay well under the ₹8 lakh lower bound, while engineered wood can push the total toward the ₹15 lakh upper bound.

durability, moisture & termite resistance

Delhi NCR experiences high humidity in monsoon and dry heat in summer. Flooring must tolerate both extremes.

Vitrified tiles

  • Water absorption < 0.5 % – practically impermeable.
  • Scratch resistance: Mohs hardness 6–7, good for high‑traffic areas.
  • Termite proof: ceramic nature offers zero food source.

Engineered wood

  • Water absorption 8–12 % – requires a moisture barrier in damp zones.
  • Scratch resistance depends on finish; high‑gloss polyurethane can mask minor dents.
  • Termite risk: core layers are resin‑bonded, but veneer can be attacked if not treated.

In practice, engineered wood performs well in living rooms and bedrooms where humidity is controlled, but it is vulnerable in basements or bathrooms.

where each option makes sense: practical scenarios

Choosing a floor is not just about numbers; it’s about where the space lives in the house.

  • Living room / dining area: High footfall, occasional spills – polished vitrified tiles provide easy cleaning and longevity.
  • Master bedroom: Comfort underfoot matters – engineered wood offers warmth and a softer feel.
  • Kitchen: Heat and oil splatter – tiles resist staining; wood needs extra sealing.
  • Home office: Noise reduction – engineered wood dampens footfall sounds better than hard tiles.

For a typical 200 sq m home in Delhi NCR, Delhi NCR, a mixed approach is common: tiles in wet zones, wood in private zones. This hybrid strategy stays within the ₹8–15 lakh range while delivering the best of both worlds.

frequently asked questions

Q1? How long does vitrified tile installation take?

For a 200 sq m area, a skilled crew typically finishes tiling in 7–10 working days, including surface preparation and grouting.

Q2? Can engineered wood be refinished?

Yes, but only if the veneer thickness exceeds 2 mm. Most residential engineered boards have a 0.6–1 mm veneer, limiting the number of refinishes.

Q3? What maintenance does each floor require?

Tiles need periodic grout cleaning and sealing of high‑traffic joints. Engineered wood requires sweeping, a damp‑mop with pH‑neutral cleaner, and re‑application of finish every 3–5 years.

Q4? Are there eco‑friendly options?

Both categories have greener variants: low‑gloss vitrified tiles with recycled content, and engineered wood made from fast‑growing species and formaldehyde‑free adhesives.
