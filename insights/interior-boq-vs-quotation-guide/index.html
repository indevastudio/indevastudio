Understanding interior BOQ vs quotation for Delhi NCR homes

The most common misconception is that a Bill of Quantities (BOQ) and a quotation are interchangeable. In reality they serve distinct purposes: a BOQ lists every material, finish, and quantity needed for a project, while a quotation translates that list into a price proposal from a contractor or supplier. Clarifying this difference early saves time, avoids budget surprises, and ensures that the Delhi NCR interior design work proceeds smoothly.

material properties

When we prepare a material boq for a residential interior, we look at each component’s physical and aesthetic characteristics. The following table summarises the most common categories we encounter in a Delhi NCR home.

  • Flooring: ceramic tiles, vitrified tiles, engineered wood, natural stone.
  • Wall finishes: gypsum plaster, acrylic paint, lime wash, textured panels.
  • Joinery: MDF, plywood, solid wood, metal frames.
  • Hardware: stainless steel hinges, brass handles, concealed hinges.
  • Ceiling: gypsum board, POP, acoustic panels.

Each material is evaluated for compressive strength, water absorption, fire rating, and visual appeal. For example, vitrified tiles typically have a water absorption rate below 0.5 %, making them suitable for high‑moisture areas like kitchens and bathrooms. In contrast, natural stone may have a higher absorption rate and requires proper sealing.

cost difference

The cost gap between a BOQ and a quotation arises from three sources:

  1. Quantity accuracy: A BOQ lists exact measurements (e.g., 45 m² of tiles). If the contractor mis‑reads the drawing, the quotation can be off by 10‑15 %.
  2. Unit price variation: Suppliers may offer discounts for bulk orders that a generic quotation does not reflect.
  3. Margin inclusion: A quotation adds the contractor’s overhead and profit, whereas a BOQ is a neutral list.

In an ILLUSTRATIVE BUDGET for a 2,500 sq ft home in Delhi NCR, the material boq might total ₹12 lakh. A contractor’s quotation, after adding labour, transport, and a 12 % margin, could rise to ₹15–18 lakh, fitting comfortably within the ₹15–25 lakh range.

durability, moisture and termite behaviour

Choosing the right material is not just about price; it also determines how the interior will age. Below are the key considerations for the Indian climate, especially in the humid parts of Delhi NCR.

Flooring

Engineered wood resists warping better than solid timber when exposed to moisture, but both need a vapor barrier in basements. Vitrified tiles are highly resistant to stains and moisture, yet they can crack under heavy impact.

Wall finishes

Gypsum plaster offers smooth finishes but is vulnerable to water seepage; it should be protected with waterproofing in wet zones. Acrylic paint provides good breathability, reducing the risk of mold growth.

Joinery

Solid wood is naturally termite‑resistant if treated, while MDF can attract termites if not sealed properly. In Delhi NCR, where termite activity is moderate, we prefer solid wood for primary furniture and MDF for secondary cabinets, always with a termiticide coating.

Even a perfectly prepared BOQ cannot compensate for a material that is unsuitable for local humidity; the right specification prevents costly rework later.

when each document makes sense

Understanding the stage of the project helps decide which document to request:

  • Concept and design phase: A BOQ helps the client visualise the scope and compare material options without committing to prices.
  • Procurement phase: A detailed quotation is essential to lock in costs, schedule deliveries, and allocate cash flow.
  • Contract negotiation: Both documents together provide a transparent basis for negotiations, reducing disputes.

For a new build in Delhi NCR, we typically hand over a material boq after the design approval. Once the client finalises the selections, we invite contractors to submit quotations based on that BOQ.

frequently asked questions

Q1: Can I use a quotation without a BOQ?

Technically you can, but you lose the ability to verify quantities and compare material alternatives. A BOQ acts as a neutral baseline.

Q2: How often do contractors change unit prices after submitting a quotation?

Changes are rare if the BOQ is accurate. Minor adjustments may occur due to market fluctuations, but they should be documented and approved.

Q3: Does a BOQ include labour costs?

No. A BOQ lists only material quantities and specifications. Labour, equipment, and contractor margins appear only in the quotation.

Q4: What should I do if the quotation exceeds my budget?

Review the BOQ to see if any material can be substituted with a cost‑effective alternative, or negotiate the contractor’s margin. Often a small change in finish can bring the total back within the ₹15–25 lakh range.
