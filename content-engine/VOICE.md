# indéva studio — writing voice

Read this before every article. The voice comes from the articles the studio wrote itself
(`/insights/execution-is-design`, `/insights/why-most-floor-plans-fail`) and the project-based
articles in `content-engine/posts/`.

## the voice in one line

a senior architect talking to a client across a site table: calm, specific, a little blunt,
never selling.

## rules

1. **lowercase.** titles, headings, body. proper nouns stay lowercase too in body text
   ("casa bianca", "narela", "dholpur stone"). acronyms in lowercase as well ("hdhmr", "rcc", "ms").
2. **short sentences.** most under 15 words. one idea per sentence. paragraphs of 2–4 sentences.
3. **open with a line that sounds like a principle**, not a summary. it becomes the pull quote.
   - good: "a farmhouse is judged by its living hall. it is decided by its ground."
   - bad: "in this article we will explore the key considerations for farmhouse design."
4. **every section earns its place with something from the project**: a decision, a constraint,
   a material, a mistake avoided. general advice only after the project evidence, never instead of it.
5. **numbered sections** as `## 01 — <claim>`. the heading is a claim, not a topic
   ("the ground was the harder problem", not "groundwater").
6. **say "we"** for the studio. never "our team of experts", never "indéva studio's skilled professionals".
7. **opinions are allowed and expected.** say what you would and would not do, and why.
8. **numbers only from the source.** sizes, timelines, counts, temperatures come from the project
   page or the brief. no prices or costs unless the brief gives them. never invent a client quote.
9. **renders are called renders.** if a project's images are design visuals, say so in the text and
   in every caption ("design render: …").
10. **end with something the reader can use**: a short checklist or the question to ask their designer,
    then one line linking the project, the matching service page and the matching city page.

## never write

- delve, tapestry, elevate, seamless, unlock, unleash, game-changer, nestled, boasts, testament,
  look no further, in today's world, whether you're, it's important to note, embark, journey (as metaphor),
  transform your space, dream home, curated (unless literal), bespoke more than once, luxurious more than once
- "in conclusion", "to sum up", "at the end of the day"
- exclamation marks, emoji, rhetorical questions in a row, title case
- claims about competitors, "best in delhi", "no.1", guarantees
- anything the source does not support

## shape

- 650–1,100 words. shorter is fine if the project has less to say. never pad.
- 4–6 numbered sections, 4–7 images from the project, each with a caption that says what the photo shows
  and ends with "<project name>, <place>."
- one primary search phrase (from the brief) used naturally in the title, the first paragraph,
  one heading and the description. never repeated more than 4 times in the body.
