## the one idea

a small office has to do three jobs in one room, so every wall is assigned a job before anything is bought. our own office in narela — one room 14'6" wide — is the worked example.

## what to draw on

- the project page: the brief (office + meeting space + sample library in one room), four layouts tried before furniture was fixed, the final plan wall by wall, the palette (olive walls, walnut veneer with 6 mm grooves, herringbone oak, black metal, white stone, rust and cane), the colour options tried for the display wall, and the drawing set (stretch ceiling, curved pop drop, cove light, 18 polo lights, 6 adjustable spots, a compact 4 ft washroom)
- angle for the reader: a business owner fitting out a small office. what to decide first (jobs per wall), why test layouts on paper, why a warm palette instead of a white box, why a full drawing set even for one room
- links: /services/commercial-interior-design and /locations/narela

## questions only the studio can answer

- was the office built from these drawings yet? if yes, which choices changed on site?
- what does the room actually feel like to work in day to day?
