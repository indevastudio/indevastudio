The brief

The hotel needed a consistent, elevated guest experience across 23 rooms and the shared lobby, lounge and meeting spaces — without the palette feeling repetitive from one room to the next.

Material strategy

A deliberately small material kit was standardised across every room and corridor: Italian marble, MDF wall mouldings and PU-finished furniture. Variation comes from lighting and layout instead, so each space reads as considered rather than templated.

Public spaces

The lobby is anchored by a handcrafted glass ceiling installation in amber and clear tones, with Chesterfield leather seating set against inlaid marble flooring. A small meeting room is finished in marble with brass-legged seating. Guest-room corridors carry wood-panelled doors and sculptural wall sconces along each floor.

Guest rooms and suites

Rooms combine herringbone flooring and PU-finished joinery at the entry and wardrobe, a tufted headboard with cane seating in the suite bedroom, and a window-side dining nook with a round table and velvet chairs. Suites include marble-clad bathrooms — one with a full jacuzzi, another with a soaking tub opening off the bedroom.

Why it matters for hospitality projects

Hotels are built for repetition: the same room, many times over. Standardising the material kit early is what keeps quality consistent across 23 rooms and keeps procurement and site execution under control.
